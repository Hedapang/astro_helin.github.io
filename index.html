Lin  He

I am currently a postdoc at the Department of Astronomy at Tsinghua University. I received my Ph.D. from the School of Astronomy and Space Science at Nanjing University under the supervision of Prof. Zhiyuan Li. Before that, I received my B.S. in Astronomy from Nanjing University in 2021.

My research focuses on the hot circumgalactic medium (CGM), galactic feedback, and active galactic nuclei (AGN). I use X-ray and multiwavelength observations to investigate the gas surrounding galaxies and the processes that shape their evolution.

Research interests

01

Hot circumgalactic medium

Tracing faint, extended hot gas in galaxy halos through deep X-ray observations and stacking analyses, from low-mass disks to Milky Way-like galaxies.

Read the study ↗

By stacking eROSITA images and spectra of nearby L* galaxies, we detect diffuse X-ray emission extending to at least 50 kpc, revealing faint halo emission that is challenging to detect in individual pointed observations. The stacked spectrum requires a substantial thermal component, supporting the presence of a hot CGM. The radial profiles compare galaxy subsamples with IllustrisTNG predictions, allowing us to investigate how stellar mass, star formation, and nuclear activity relate to the distribution of hot gas.

02

Galactic Winds & Feedback

Investigating large-scale bubbles, galactic outflows, and the interaction between star formation, nuclear activity, and the surrounding gas.

Read the study ↗

Multiwavelength observations of NGC 6286 reveal a pair of giant bubbles with a projected radius of about 5 kpc. The close alignment of soft X-rays and Hα emission traces the interaction between a hot wind and cooler swept-up gas. The current starburst can supply enough energy to inflate the bubbles, although a contribution from past AGN activity cannot be excluded. This system provides a nearby laboratory for studying how feedback transports energy into a galaxy’s halo.

03

AGN in interacting galaxies

Exploring the connection between galaxy interactions and black hole accretion, including X-ray constraints on active nuclei in close galaxy pairs.

Read the study ↗

Our Chandra survey examines nuclear X-ray emission in 92 optically selected close (projected separation < 20 kpc) galaxy pairs. This figure compares nuclear luminosity as a function of projected pair separation with that of AGN-pair and control samples. Close interaction alone does not guarantee strong black hole accretion (as suggested in the classical picture): the smallest-separation nuclei often have unexpectedly low X-ray luminosities and Eddington ratios. These measurements help constrain the connection between galaxy interactions, gas supply, and nuclear activity.
